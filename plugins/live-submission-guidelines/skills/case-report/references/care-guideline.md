# CARE guideline — the content backbone

CARE (**CA**se **RE**port) is the international reporting standard for case
reports (Gagnier et al., 2013; part of the EQUATOR network). It defines *what a
complete case report contains*, independent of any journal's house style. Use it
as the completeness backbone so a report is thorough regardless of venue; the
venue file then dictates *format* (word limit, headings, reference style) on top.

The point of leaning on CARE rather than an ad-hoc template: it keeps the skill
general across specialties and journals instead of overfitting to one example.

## CARE checklist items (full manuscript)

1. **Title** — names it as a case report and states the phenomenon of interest.
2. **Keywords** — 2–5.
3. **Abstract** — background; case summary; conclusion / take-home.
4. **Introduction** — brief background and why this case matters, with 1–2
   references.
5. **Patient information** — de-identified demographics, chief complaint,
   relevant history.
6. **Clinical findings** — examination and relevant findings.
7. **Timeline** — key dates/events as *relative* timing (de-identified).
8. **Diagnostic assessment** — methods, reasoning, differential, challenges.
9. **Therapeutic intervention** — type, administration, changes, with rationale.
10. **Follow-up and outcomes** — clinician- and patient-assessed outcomes,
    adverse events, and honest limits of follow-up.
11. **Discussion** — strengths/limitations, relevant literature, rationale for
    conclusions, the take-home lesson.
12. **Patient perspective** — when appropriate.
13. **Informed consent** — statement that consent was obtained (flag as a
    clinician-input item if not yet confirmed).

## Mapping CARE onto the two modes

**Abstract mode** compresses CARE into three moves:

- *Introduction* ← items 1, 4 (why this case matters)
- *Case Presentation* ← items 5–10 (the de-identified course, tightly)
- *Discussion & Conclusions* ← item 11 (+ the single take-home)

Consent and timeline still matter — they just live in the clinician-input
checklist rather than as prose when the word limit is tight.

**Manuscript mode** expands to the full item list under the venue's section
hierarchy, with figures/tables for items 6–10 where the records support them.

## Honesty about outcomes and follow-up

CARE explicitly asks for adverse events and the limits of follow-up. Resist the
pull to imply a smooth course when follow-up is short — write "follow-up here is
short-term" rather than overclaiming a durable result. An honest limitation is a
strength a reviewer respects; an implied outcome you can't support is a finding
the adversarial pass (Stage 5) should catch.
